# Session 10 November 2026: scaling to bigger images

Week 5 of the handoff plan (handoff given 12 October). T01 is still open.

## 1. Questions discussed

- **Tensor networks.** The repo already uses quimb for 2×L strips (`ladder_tensor.py`),
  the local Petz channel (`local_petz_tensor.py`), and the old Algorithm 3 MPS backend.
  For thermal correlations on 2D images, SSE quantum Monte Carlo is the better tool:
  this Hamiltonian has no sign problem and MC cost grows about linearly with N, while
  an MPS needs bond dimension exponential in image width. Tensor networks are most
  useful for simulating the quantum circuit itself and for cross-checks on strips.
- **Bigger images.** The samplers and the seed readout rules scale fine. The neural
  "volume" decoder takes the full N×N correlation matrix, so it does not scale past
  roughly 16×16 without a redesign (PI decision).
- **C++.** Unlikely to help: the hot loops already use numba, and the tensor work
  spends its time in compiled BLAS. Profile first.

## 2. Scaling benchmark

`algorithm1/python/benchmark_sizes.py` (not yet committed) runs the Ising Gibbs sampler
and the foreground-seed sign rule C(F,i) > 0 on a synthetic noisy disk at 8, 16, 32 and
64 pixels per side. Output: `algorithm1/results/student-scaling/results.json` and
`sizes.png`.

| Side | N | Time (s) | IoU | Max R-hat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 64 | 0.8 | 0.958 | 1.12 |
| 16 | 256 | 3.0 | 1.000 | 1.32 |
| 32 | 1024 | 12.6 | 0.992 | 1.26 |
| 64 | 4096 | 50.5 | 0.994 | 1.44 |

**What it shows:** runtime is linear in N at a fixed sample budget (log-log slope 0.99).

**What it does not show:**
- **Convergence.** R-hat should be below 1.05; every size fails. The times are for a
  fixed budget (512 burn-in, 1024 samples, 2 chains), not time to convergence.
- **Segmentation quality.** The disk and background differ by 150 intensity units with
  noise SD 30 and τ = 100, so almost every edge has the right sign before sampling.
- **A realistic temperature.** b ≈ 45 (β_paper up to 1.8 × 10⁵); Γ sits near ±0.25,
  essentially the ground state.

Label it as a scaling check, not a segmentation result.
